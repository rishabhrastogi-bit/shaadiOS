Execution plan

Roadmap

Full-time founder. Solo. 6–12 month personal runway. Every decision measured against one question: does this get closer to a paying subscriber? First revenue by month 8 at the latest.

"Build only what gets you to a paying subscriber. Nothing else."

You have 6–12 months of runway. That is enough — if you validate before building, hire only when blocked, and treat every week without a paying user as a week of runway burned. The phases below are gates, not timelines. Move to the next phase only when the gate conditions are met — not when the calendar says so.

Phase 0
Foundation
Weeks 1–2 · ₹1–1.5L · Self-funded
📋 Week 1 — Legal & structure
Register Pvt Ltd company — not sole prop, not LLP. Pvt Ltd lets you issue equity to a co-founder later and creates clean IP ownership. Use Razorpay Rize or a CA. Cost: ₹15,000–25,000. Time: 7–10 working days.

Commission IRDAI legal opinion — find an insurance regulation specialist (not a general lawyer). Ask specifically: does our subscription structure constitute insurance under the Insurance Act 1938? Budget ₹75,000–1,00,000. This is not optional — it is the foundation of the entire subscription model.

Secure the domain — marriageos.in or getmarriageos.com. ₹800–1,500/year. Deploy the pre-signup page on Netlify with password protection for now.
🤝 Week 2 — People & conversations
Send NDA to 3 trusted people — the ones you'd consider as co-founders or early advisors. Share the Founder OS. Schedule one 60-min call each. You are not seeking validation. You are looking for one person who gets it immediately and wants in.

What you need from them: a technical co-founder with React Native or Flutter experience removes your single biggest build dependency. If none of your 3 know mobile development, ask them to refer someone.

Set up infrastructure: business email, Notion workspace, GitHub account for eventual codebase, private Netlify URL for the Founder OS.
👤 Hiring at this phase
No hires yet. Do not spend money on people before validation. Your only spend is legal (IRDAI opinion, company registration). If you hire a developer now you will build the wrong thing.
💰 Funding at this phase
Self-fund only. You have runway. Nobody will fund you at this stage — nor should they. Your only job is to get to validation data. Any investor conversation now is premature and distracts from the work.
✅ Gate: Company registered · Legal opinion commissioned · NDA sent to 3 people · Pre-signup page live

The three things that will kill this if you let them
⚠️ Building before validating
The temptation to start coding in month 1 is real and strong. Resist it completely. Every week you spend building before 5 people have said "I would pay for this" is a week of runway burned on the wrong thing. Phase 1 exists entirely to prevent this mistake.
⚠️ Targeting everyone simultaneously
NRIs only for the first 6 months. When you have 30 NRI subscribers you can think about expanding to domestic urban. Not before. Every time you say "this product is for all Indian couples," you are making it harder to find your first 50 customers.
⚠️ Staying solo too long
Full-time founder, 6-month runway, no technical help. By month 3 you need at least one other person committed. A solo founder with no technical co-founder and a ticking runway clock makes increasingly desperate decisions. Start the co-founder search in week 1, not month 3.
The idea

MarriageOS

India's first marriage protection platform — starting as an app, evolving into a subscription, arriving as insurance. Built for the 10 million Indian couples who marry every year with zero financial protection if it breaks down.

"You insure your car, your home, your health.
Why not your marriage?"

Prenuptial agreements are legally unenforceable in India. There are zero financial protection products for marital breakdown. Yet 500,000–700,000 divorce cases are filed annually, each one costing ₹3–15 lakh in legal fees and 2–5 years in courts. For NRIs, add cross-border chaos, mandatory India travel, and jurisdictional battles across two legal systems. MarriageOS fills this vacuum — starting with an app, proven with subscriptions, licensed as insurance.

Who it's for
🇮🇳 Urban Indian Couples
Dual-income, metro-based, 25–40. Financially independent. Growing awareness that divorce is real. No product exists for them. TAM: 30–40M couples.
✈️ NRIs (Primary Beachhead)
32M NRIs globally. India-linked marriages. Cross-border legal chaos is their specific nightmare. Highest WTP. Word of mouth travels fast in diaspora communities.
💰 HNI Couples
High-asset couples where divorce risk is financially catastrophic. Property, business interests, international assets. Willing to pay ₹45K+/yr for dedicated case management.
Why now
58% of Indian millennials now find divorce acceptable
Up from 32% in 2010 (Pew, 2023). Social stigma is dropping. Demand for protection products will follow the attitude shift — just as health insurance followed health awareness.
Women's financial independence is accelerating divorce rates
Female labour force participation rising. 70% of urban divorces now initiated by women. The party that files needs protection products too — not just the respondent.
Courts are overwhelmed — 2–5 year divorce timelines
56M+ pending cases nationally. Contested divorces drag for years. Every day of delay is financial and emotional cost — exactly what a protection product is built to absorb.
No competitor has attempted this in India
WedLock tried in the US. China Taiping tried in China. Both failed — but as pure insurance products. The app-first, data-led, subscription-bridge approach is new. The window is open.
App features

MarriageOS App

The seed of the ecosystem. Three views of the same feature set — by build layer, by monetisation, and by where it sits in a couple's life journey.

🟢 Core MVP — Build first. Without these the app doesn't exist.
👫
Dual Couple Login
Two separate accounts, one linked couple profile. Each partner sees their own private space AND shared couple data. Neither can see the other's private entries unless explicitly shared. Critical — this is not a shared family account. Each person retains individual identity.
CoreFree
💡 Founder note — Onboarding status selection
First screen after signup shows a simple, warm selection: "Are you married or planning to marry soon?" — two clear options, not a dropdown, not a form. This single choice routes the entire onboarding experience. Married → couple-linking flow, calendar setup, health tracker intro. Getting married soon → pre-marriage setup, asset baseline option, inspiration content for new couples. This also immediately segments the user for product analytics and eventual insurance pre-signup targeting. Do not ask for relationship status mid-app — ask it first, frame it positively, make it feel like personalisation not interrogation.
💡 Founder note — KYC (Know Your Customer)
Light KYC is essential — this is a trust and safety product. Fake users pollute the health data that eventually powers underwriting. Recommended approach: mobile OTP verification at signup (mandatory, free, instant — confirms real phone number). For subscription tier: Aadhaar-based e-KYC or DigiLocker — verifies identity in under 60 seconds, fully digital, no document upload friction. For NRI tier: passport number + selfie verification via a KYC API (Signzy, Hyperverge, or IDFY — all integrate easily, cost ₹10–25 per verification). Do not ask both partners to complete KYC at signup — verify the primary account holder first, let the partner join via invite link with OTP only. Full dual KYC can be required at subscription upgrade. Keep the free tier frictionless — OTP only — to maximise top-of-funnel conversion.
📅
Life Calendar & Logging (Flo-style)
The daily-use hook. Couples log anything — birthdays, anniversaries, conflicts, milestones, medical events, fights. Each entry gets time + place tag. Entries can be flagged as private, shared, or saved to the document vault. This is what makes it a daily-use app, not a crisis-only app. Inspired directly by how Flo creates habit around women's health tracking.
CoreFree (basic) / Paid (vault save)
💡 Founder note — Quick-toggle logging
Add a row of small toggle buttons on the daily log screen — pre-set event chips the user configures themselves (e.g. "Good day", "Had a fight", "Date night", "Feeling distant", "Grateful moment", "Stressful at work"). One tap on any chip logs it instantly to that day in the calendar — no typing, no opening a new screen. User can customise which chips appear. This is exactly how Flo handles symptom logging — tap once, it's done, you move on. Creates the daily habit with near-zero friction. Logged chips become pattern data over time (e.g. "you've had 4 'feeling distant' days this month") that feeds the quarterly health check-in with real signal rather than just self-reported answers.
💚
Marriage Health Tracker
Quarterly structured check-in. 15 questions, both partners answer independently. App surfaces divergence — where you align and where you don't — without judgment. Early warning system. Not therapy. Think of it as a quarterly "relationship audit." Generates longitudinal data that eventually powers insurance underwriting.
CoreFree (summary) / Paid (full report)
🌸
Relationship Inspiration Feed
The heart of the app's daily experience. A curated, ever-refreshing feed of content that makes couples feel good about their relationship — not anxious about its risks. Stories of couples who navigated hard times and came out stronger. Research-backed suggestions for deepening connection. Weekly prompts: "Do you remember the last time you surprised each other?" Positive, warm, human. Think Headspace for marriages — not a law library.

Why this is not insurance (and why that matters)
No IRDAI license required
This is a membership/retainer service — not an insurance product. Members pay for access to services and a pre-agreed cost coverage cap. No risk pooling, no formal "claim" mechanism. Legal structuring review by a lawyer (one-time cost ~₹75,000) confirms this classification before launch.
No "claims" — only usage caps
Instead of claim ratios, we manage cost through service caps per tier. A high-usage member (3 lawyer calls, 2 mediator sessions) is not a "claimant" — they're a high-engagement subscriber. Peace-of-mind buyers (50%+ of base) use zero services. This is how LegalShield in the US operates profitably.
Moral hazard managed by design
Legal cost coverage only activates when proceedings are filed AGAINST the member — not when they self-initiate. This removes the primary moral hazard (paying to trigger the event). It's the same principle as "uninsured motorist" coverage — you're protected against someone else's action.
Revenue model proven before IRDAI
By the time we approach an insurer partner in Year 3, we walk in with 20,000+ paying subscribers, a proven ₹8–18K ARPU, real usage data, and a behavioural underwriting model. That's the difference between a pitch and a partnership.
Subscription rules — usage caps & lock-in
⏰ Lock-in period & purchase deadline
Annual commitment: Subscription is purchased for a full year — no monthly option at launch. This reduces churn, improves cash flow predictability, and filters out people who join only when already in crisis (adverse selection control).

Purchase deadline / waiting period: Coverage and professional services activate 90 days after purchase. Someone cannot buy on the day their spouse serves papers and immediately claim services. The 90-day window mirrors standard insurance waiting periods and is the single most important moral hazard control in the subscription model.

Renewal window: Auto-renews 30 days before expiry. Member gets a 14-day window to cancel before renewal triggers. No refund after renewal processes.

💡 Ideal sales moment: wedding season (Oct–Feb). Couples buy at marriage — 90-day wait expires well before any realistic crisis window. Feels like responsible planning, not fear.
🤝 Mediation — 2 sessions / 2 issues per year
Cap: 2 mediation sessions per subscription year. Each session tied to a declared issue — prevents sessions being used as general venting or extended therapy substitutes.

Session length: 90 minutes per session (standard mediation format).

Who conducts: Empanelled certified mediators only — not lawyers, not therapists. Separate professional category.

Additional sessions: Available at ₹3,500–5,000 per session (negotiated empanelment rate, margin for MarriageOS).

Unit economics: Mediator cost to MarriageOS ~₹2,000–2,500/session at empanelment rates. 2 sessions = ₹4,000–5,000 cost against ₹8,000 subscription. If 40% of subscribers use both sessions, blended delivery cost is manageable. If 60%+ use both, review pricing.
💬 Therapy — 3 sessions per year
Cap: 3 therapy sessions per subscription year. Couples therapy only — not individual therapy. Both partners must attend.

Session length: 50 minutes per session (standard therapy hour).

Who conducts: Empanelled licensed therapists / relationship counsellors. Not mediators, not lawyers.

Additional sessions: Available at ₹2,500–4,000 per session depending on therapist tier.

Unit economics: Therapist cost to MarriageOS ~₹1,500–2,000/session at empanelment rates. 3 sessions = ₹4,500–6,000 cost. If 30% of subscribers use all 3 sessions, blended delivery cost is ₹1,350–1,800 per subscriber — well within margin.

💡 Positioning note: never call these "therapy sessions" in marketing. Call them "relationship conversations with a professional." Therapy carries stigma in India. Conversation does not.

⚠️ Actuarial caveat — adverse selection

Build stack

Technology

How to build MarriageOS — a beautiful, lifetime-companion app like Flo — using an AI-assisted stack. iOS + Android from day one. Full code ownership. ~10x cheaper than hiring a developer, with your time as the trade.

"Design in AI. Generate real code. Own everything."

The 2026 AI tooling is genuinely good enough to take a non-developer founder from idea to a working, ownable mobile app. No single tool does it all — the winning approach is a chain: AI design → AI code generation → AI-assisted custom logic → managed backend. React Native (what Flo itself uses) is the right foundation because it ships to both app stores from one codebase and every feature MarriageOS needs already exists as standard components.

Recommended stack
📱 Framework — React Native + Expo
Same framework that powers Flo, Discord, Shopify. One codebase → iOS + Android. Expo removes painful native setup, handles app store submission, push notifications, camera, storage, and over-the-air updates. Deep developer talent pool in India if you ever hire. Every MarriageOS feature — calendar, toggle chips, dual login, vault, health tracker — is a standard component. You are building a beautiful consumer app, not anything exotic.
🗄️ Backend — Supabase
Postgres database + built-in auth (OTP for KYC) + row-level security (keeps each partner's private entries invisible to the other) + file storage (document vault) + edge functions (health score calc, SOS routing). Free tier covers first 500+ users. Indian data residency available for compliance. No AI needed here — it just works out of the box.
🔐 KYC & Auth — Supabase Auth + Signzy/Hyperverge
Supabase Auth handles free-tier OTP login. Layer Signzy or Hyperverge for Aadhaar e-KYC at subscription upgrade (~60 sec, fully digital). For international tier: passport + selfie verification via IDFY. All have React Native SDKs. Cost: ₹10–25 per verification. Keep free tier to OTP-only to maximise top-of-funnel conversion.
📊 Analytics + Notifications
Mixpanel tracks which features users open, drop-off points, DAU/WAU — essential for validating the daily-use hypothesis. Expo Notifications for basic push; upgrade to OneSignal for segmented campaigns. Documents in vault need AES-256 encryption at rest — non-negotiable for a trust product.

Three things to copy from Flo architecturally
The log IS the home screen
Open Flo → you're already on today's date with toggle chips visible. No navigation to logging. MarriageOS home = calendar + today + quick-toggle chips. Not a dashboard. Not a feed. The log. This is the daily habit trigger.
Passive data collection

Flo shows content relevant to your cycle stage. MarriageOS shows content relevant to your relationship stage — newlyweds see different Journal content than couples in year 7. Tag content by stage, filter by profile. Build this from day one even with a small library.
Founder design vision — the MarriageOS feeling
"Indian tradition, held in pastel. Peace and love as a couple — from the very first screen."
The visual identity should root itself in the warmth and sacredness of Indian marriage tradition — but rendered in soft, modern pastels rather than loud, saturated festival colours. The feeling is not a wedding invitation. It is the calm, enduring love of two people who chose each other. Traditional soul, contemporary calm.
🎨 Palette — Indian tradition in pastel
Draw from the traditional Indian marriage palette but soften every colour to a pastel:

• Haldi yellow → soft buttermilk (warmth, auspicious beginnings)
• Sindoor red → dusty rose (love, union — never alarming red)
• Mehendi green → sage (growth, life together)
• Sky/turquoise → soft powder blue (trust, calm, the divine)
• Gold accents → matte champagne (sacredness, used sparingly as highlights)

The existing MarriageOS palette (sage, rose, sky, warm white) already aligns — extend it with buttermilk and champagne-gold accents to complete the traditional-in-pastel feel.
💑 Welcome screen — the eternal couple
The first thing a couple sees on opening the app: a warm, illustrated (not photographic) Indian bride and groom — expressing an eternal, tender bond. Style: soft, minimal line-art or gentle watercolour, not ornate or busy. Think a modern, elegant illustration — not a clip-art wedding card.

The imagery should feel inclusive across regions and communities (avoid over-indexing on one specific regional wedding style). Convey partnership and devotion — two people turned slightly toward each other, calm, whole. Pair with a gentle welcome line that sets the tone of the entire product.
🌸 Design elements — peace & love motifs
Weave in subtle, culturally resonant design elements that signal peace and devotion without clutter:

• Soft mandala or rangoli-inspired geometry as gentle background texture (very low opacity)
• Lotus motif — peace, purity, unfolding love — as a recurring quiet symbol
• Intertwined / paired elements (two diyas, two birds, two lotuses) reinforcing the couple as a unit
• Flowing, rounded forms — no sharp corners anywhere

Restraint is key. One or two motifs used consistently, not a festival of ornament. The tradition should whisper, not shout.
💛 Daily love thought — the returning ritual
Each day, greet the couple with a simple, warm relationship thought — a single line that makes them pause and feel their bond. Not advice. Not a task. A gentle daily touch.

Examples of tone: "The smallest 'I noticed you' can mean more than the grandest gift." · "Today, remember one reason you chose each other."

This is the emotional equivalent of Flo's daily open — a reason to return that feels like care, not obligation. Rotate from a curated library; eventually personalise by relationship stage. Position it on the home/log screen so it's the first warm thing they read each day.

Traditional soul (lotus, subtle rangoli geometry, paired motifs, champagne-gold accents) rendered with contemporary calm and lots of breathing space. Global competitors — who tried marriage insurance
WedLock / Marriage Assurances
USA · Founded ~2008 · Still technically operating but never scaled
Failed to scale
World's first divorce insurance product. Required 4-year waiting period before payouts. US divorce rate is 40%+ — yet the product never became mainstream. Moral hazard problem was never structurally solved. Adverse selection (only stressed couples buy) made pricing impossible. Eventually rebranded but remains niche.
Pure insurance without behavioural data is unworkable. The waiting period killed willingness to buy. App-first approach solves both problems.
China Taiping — Marriage Insurance
China · Launched 2016 · Quietly discontinued
Discontinued
Structured as a savings-cum-protection hybrid. Launched with some publicity. Regulators flagged moral hazard concerns. Without data to prove actuarial viability, the product couldn't get sustained regulatory support. Never expanded beyond pilot stage.
Regulators need data to approve novel products. The subscription phase builds us the data set we need before any IRDAI conversation.
LegalShield (USA)
USA · Founded 1972 · $500M+ revenue · Profitable · Adjacent model
Adjacent — learn from
Legal subscription service — not insurance. Members pay monthly for access to a network of lawyers. Pre-paid legal consultations, document review, representation at discounted rates. 1.7M+ members. Profitable for 50+ years. The proof that a legal subscription model works at scale before insurance is even attempted.
Our subscription model is directly inspired by LegalShield. The key insight: members who never use services (peace-of-mind buyers) are your most profitable customers. Target them aggressively.
Shaadi.com / Jeevansathi / BharatMatrimony
India · Large incumbents · Matrimonial platforms
Distribution channel
These platforms know who just got married AND who is in relationship stress. They have the data, the reach, and the user intent. They are NOT protection products — but they could be powerful distribution partners or acquirers. A partnership with any one of them gives us wedding-moment access to millions of couples.
Don't compete with matrimonial platforms — partner with them. They need post-marriage engagement. We need pre-marriage distribution. Perfect alignment.
iDivorce / Indian legal subscription services
India · Emerging · Ad hoc legal service platforms
Indirect competitor
A handful of Indian startups offer on-demand legal services for matrimonial matters — document prep, lawyer matching, process guidance. All transactional. None have a protection/prevention layer. None have the app engagement model. None are building toward insurance.
The market is validating legal service demand. We differentiate on: (1) prevention not just cure, (2) subscription not transaction, (3) insurance as the destination. Nobody is building the stack we're building.
The gap we fill
No one owns the full stack: App → Subscription → Insurance → Data
Every competitor is either a single-point service (legal help when you call), a failed pure-insurance product, or an unrelated matrimonial platform. The full-stack protection product — built app-first, proven subscription-second, licensed insurance-third — does not exist in India. The window is open. The question is only how fast we can validate and move.
Hypotheses

What this business lives or dies on

Each card is a critical assumption. Click the status badge to cycle through: Untested → Testing → Validated → Killed. Add evidence as you go.

NRI couples will pay ₹12,000–18,000/year for a cross-border marriage protection plan
This is the primary revenue hypothesis. NRIs face the highest pain (cross-border legal chaos, mandatory India travel, dual jurisdictions) and have demonstrated ability to pay for professional services. The price point is ~1 month's professional salary for most NRI target customers. If this fails, the NRI beachhead fails.
Get 50 NRI pre-signups from organic LinkedIn/WhatsApp outreach within 30 days. Follow up with 10 discovery calls. Ask directly if they'd pay ₹15,000/year.
Couples will use the app weekly (not just in crisis) if the calendar/logging feature is the daily hook
This is the engagement hypothesis. The entire data moat depends on daily/weekly app usage — not crisis-only opens. If couples only open the app when in trouble, we get sparse data, poor retention, and no behavioural underwriting model. The Flo-app parallel: women open Flo daily not because they're ill but because it's a habit. We need the same.
Build a 30-day prototype of the calendar feature. Give it to 20 couples. Measure weekly active usage at day 7, 14, 30. Target: 50%+ open the app at least once/week.
The subscription model avoids IRDAI classification as an insurance product
This is the legal/regulatory hypothesis. The entire Phase 1 and 2 plan rests on operating as a membership service, not an insurer. A single legal challenge from IRDAI reclassifying our product could shut down the subscription tier before we have the capital or license to respond. Must be confirmed by a qualified insurance lawyer before launch.
Commission a legal opinion from a lawyer specialising in IRDAI regulation. Budget ₹75,000. Must be done before first paid subscriber.
An Indian insurer will partner with us in Year 3 if we bring 20,000+ subscribers and behavioural data
This is the insurance destination hypothesis. The entire long-term thesis depends on a licensed insurer being willing to white-label or co-develop a marriage insurance product using our data. If no insurer is willing, we are capped at a subscription business. Need to begin informal conversations with insurer BD teams in Year 1-2, not Year 3.
Get one informal conversation with a BD contact at ICICI Lombard or Bajaj Allianz in the next 6 months. Test the concept. Gauge regulatory appetite.
Mediation before litigation saves couples ₹5–20 lakh — and they know this
The mediation-first narrative is a core marketing message. If couples don't understand that mediation can save years and lakhs, they won't value it as a subscription feature. They'll see it as a "softer" option and skip it for lawyers — defeating the cost-prevention purpose.
Write one content piece with real cost comparison data (mediation vs litigation). Publish on LinkedIn. Measure engagement and comments. Are people surprised by the numbers?
Both spouses will sign up together (not just one defensive partner)
The product works best as a joint purchase — like a joint life insurance policy. If only one spouse buys it (typically the more anxious one), the other may see it as adversarial ("you're planning our divorce"). The framing must position it as mutual protection, not self-protection. This is both a product design and marketing challenge.
In discovery calls, ask: "Would you buy this alone, or would both of you need to sign up?" If 70%+ say "both of us," the joint framing works. If majority say "just me," reconsider the product model.
Competitive moat

What makes this defensible

Six structural advantages that compound over time. The longer we operate, the harder each becomes to replicate.

🧠
Behavioural Data Moat — The most powerful, most time-dependent
Three years of quarterly marriage health check-in data across thousands of couples creates an underwriting dataset no insurer has ever had access to. Competitors cannot buy this data — it can only be built through consistent user engagement over years. This is the core negotiating chip with any insurer partner. And it gets stronger every month we operate.
🤝
Trust Moat — Crisis products require trust built in calm times
This is a product people buy hoping never to use — like life insurance. The brand that is trusted in calm times gets called in crisis. Trust is built through content, through professional empanelment quality, through founder credibility (you have lived the NRI family law experience), and through user reviews from couples who never needed to make a claim. This trust cannot be manufactured with ad spend.
👥
Professional Network Moat — The empanelled layer is hard to replicate
A vetted, reviewed, available network of family lawyers, mediators, and therapists across India and key NRI geographies takes years to build. Every professional we empanel is a relationship. Every successful referral strengthens the two-sided network. A competitor can launch an app in 6 months — they cannot replicate 200 empanelled professionals overnight.
🏛️
Regulatory First-Mover Moat — Being first in a new IRDAI category
If and when IRDAI approves a marriage breakdown insurance product, the first licensed product gets years of exclusivity in practice — regulators approve cautiously, and second movers face a market where the first brand already owns "marriage insurance" in the consumer's mind. This is the same dynamic that benefited the first health insurance products in India in the 1990s.
🌏
NRI Distribution Moat — Community word of mouth is irreplaceable
NRI communities are tight-knit. A product that helps one NRI family through a legal crisis gets recommended to every couple in their network within weeks. This word-of-mouth flywheel is nearly impossible to replicate with paid acquisition alone. The founder's own NRI experience (Belgium, Luxembourg, France, US) is a unique credibility asset in these communities.
💚
Brand Moat — Owning "marriage protection" in India
There is no brand in India that owns this category. The first serious entrant with quality execution, founder credibility, and consistent content gets to define what "marriage protection" means. Brand in trust-based categories (insurance, legal, healthcare) is deeply sticky. Once we own this mental real estate, a well-funded competitor entering later faces a "second bank" problem — consumers already have one they trust.
